Kishkindha Kanda

Rama's alliance with Sugriva and Hanuma.

Illustrated overview from the source manuscript.

RAMAYANAM

Kishkindha Kanda

❈

Rama and Laxmana walked towards Rishyamuka Parvatam. The spring blossoms and Coupled nature reminded Rama intensely of Sita, causing him to lament. Laxmana Encouraged him, noting that grief dims valor and that Rama’s peerless archery Would conquer any enemy across the three worlds.

Perched high on Rishyamuka, Sugriva spotted the two warriors bearing heavy bows And feared they were assassins sent by his brother Vali. Hanuma counseled Calmness and offered to investigate in disguise.

Hanuma disguised himself as a mendicant monk (Bhikshuvu) and approached them. Although an ascetic traditionally receives homage from householders, Hanuma Instinctively bowed to Rama, perceiving the divine presence of Lord Vishnu. He Addressed them in eloquent, grammatically flawless Sanskrit. Rama remarked to Laxmana that only a master of the Vedas and Upanishads could speak with such Elegance and restraint.

Hanuma revealed his true form, placed Rama and Laxmana on his broad shoulders, And flew up the crags to Sugriva. Before a consecrated fire kindled by Hanuma, Rama and Sugriva clasped hands, circumambulated the flame, and forged a sacred Pact of mutual alliance. Auspicious omens stirred: Sita’s left eye fluttered in Lanka, while the left eyes of Vali and Ravana twitched unfavorably.

Sugriva brought out the bundle of ornaments dropped by Sita from the sky. Rama Recognized them through tears and showed them to Laxmana. Laxmana remarked that He could not identify the armlets or earrings, recognizing only the anklets (Nupura) because he bowed daily at Sita’s feet and never raised his eyes to her Face.

Sugriva related his past. He and Vali had lived harmoniously until a demon named Mayavi challenged Vali. Vali chased Mayavi into a subterranean cavern, ordering Sugriva to guard the mouth. Sugriva waited a full year. Hearing demon roars and Seeing frothy blood emerge, he concluded Vali was slain. To prevent the demon From overrunning Kishkinda, he blocked the entrance with a boulder and returned Home. The ministers crowned Sugriva king.

Vali returned alive, flew into a rage, denounced Sugriva as a traitor, struck Him, and expelled him. Vali then unlawfully claimed Ruma, Sugriva’s wife, as his Own. Sugriva fled to Rishyamuka Parvatam, the one sanctuary forbidden to Vali due To a curse by Sage Matanga (after Vali had hurled the bloody carcass of the demon Dundubhi near his ashram).

To dispel Sugriva’s lingering doubts regarding his ability to face the formidable Vali, Rama demonstrated his martial strength. He casually flicked the skeletal Remains of Dundubhi a hundred miles with his toe. Furthermore, with a single arrow, Rama pierced through seven massive Sala trees, the underlying hill, and into the Earth, the arrow returning neatly to his quiver.

Reassured, Sugriva challenged Vali at Kishkinda’s gates. The brothers engaged in Furious wrestling. Standing concealed behind trees, Rama could not distinguish Between the two identical vanaras and withheld his shot. Sugriva, battered and Bruised, fled back to the mountain.

Rama clarified his dilemma and instructed Laxmana to place a flowering Gaja-pushpi Garland around Sugriva’s neck for identification. Sugriva roared a second challenge. When Vali strode forth, his wise wife Tara urged caution, noting that Sugriva had Returned emboldened, having formed an alliance with Prince Rama. Vali rebuffed Her warning, declaring that Rama was a righteous soul who would never commit an Unrighteous act, and met Sugriva in combat.

As Sugriva began to falter, Rama discharged a penetrating shaft that tore through Vali’s chest, felling him. Approaching the fallen warrior, Vali censured Rama for Striking from concealment like a coward. Rama responded firmly:

  • A ruler may hunt an animal using concealment.
  • Vali had committed an unpardonable sin by taking the wife of his living younger Brother, an offense punishable by death under royal dharma.

Vali recognized his fault, surrendered, and entrusted his young son Angada to Rama and Sugriva. Handing his divine golden chain over to Sugriva, Vali passed Away peacefully.

Following the obsequies, Sugriva was crowned King of Kishkinda and Angada was Invested as Crown Prince. Rama, committed to his forest vow, declined to enter The capital and retreated to the Prasravana mountain caves for the four rainy Months, giving Sugriva leave to enjoy his realm.

Immersed in palace indulgences, Sugriva lost track of time. Prompted by Hanuma, Sugriva dispatched Commander Neela to summon all vanara hosts from across the Earth within fifteen days (Sugrivagyna). However, Sugriva continued his revels Beyond the deadline.

Enraged at the lapse, Rama sent Laxmana to Kishkinda. Laxmana twanged his bowstring At the gates, shaking the palace. Tara met him with respectful words, pacifying His fury. She ushered Laxmana inside, where a sobered Sugriva prostrated, begged Pardon, and mobilized the arriving armies.

Sugriva divided millions of vanara troops into four divisions, sending them to the North, South, East, and West, giving them a strict one-month limit to discover Sita’s whereabouts. Entrusting the crucial southern route to Hanuma, Angada, and Jambavan, Rama gave Hanuma his personal signet ring (Anguleeyakam) to present to Sita as a token of identity.

The northern, eastern, and western search parties returned unsuccessful within The month. The southern group searched through the Vindhya caves and entered the Parched, desolate territory cursed by Sage Kandu. Suffering from thirst and Hunger, they discovered a subterranean cave, holding hands to navigate the Darkness.

The cavern opened into a radiant subterranean realm crafted by Maya Danava, Featuring crystal palaces and golden lotus pools. There they met the ascetic Swayamprabha, who fed them and, learning of their mission for Rama, used her Mystical power to transport them instantly to the southern seashore.

Finding that four months had passed beyond their deadline, Angada and the vanaras Despaired, preparing for death through fasting (Prayopavesam). Nearby, the Wingless vulture Sampati, elder brother of Jatayuvu, overheard them discussing Jatayuvu’s sacrifice. Sampati explained that his wings had been scorched long ago When he shielded Jatayuvu from the sun’s scorching rays. Blessed with far-reaching Vision, Sampati gazed across the southern sea and revealed: • Sita was interned one hundred yojanas away in Lanka, seated under a Simsupa Tree in the Asoka Vanam. • Indra secretly provided her divine payasam to keep her alive.

Having shared this news for Rama’s cause, Sampati’s wings regenerated instantly.

Gathering on the seashore, the vanara leaders assessed their jumping capacities. None possessed the strength to leap one hundred yojanas and return. Jambavan Stepped forward and reminded Hanuma of his divine birth and dormant power. Hanuma Expanded his form into a colossus, mounted the Mahendra mountain, and prepared To leap across the ocean.
