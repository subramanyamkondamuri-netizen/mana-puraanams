                                 9. PRASNOPANISHAD

Belonging to the Atharvaveda, this text consists of six chapters and 67 mantras. Six advanced seekers of
truth approached Sage Pippalada, carrying sacred sacrificial wood (Samidhas) in token of their humility
and readiness to serve. Pippalada requested that they reside in his hermitage for one year observing
austerity, self-control, and faith. At the end of the year, they posed six fundamental questions.


THE SIX INQUIRIES & SAGE PIPPALADA'S REVELATIONS

1. Origin of Living Creatures (Kabandhi Katyayana's Question)
"From where do all these living creatures originate?"

Pippalada revealed: The Creator meditated and produced a primal polarity: Rayi (passive, form-bearing
matter, symbolized by the Moon) and Prana (active, conscious energy, symbolized by the Sun). The Sun
represents life and dynamic energy; the Moon represents physical form and matter. Through their cosmic
interplay, all species are brought into existence.

2. The Sustaining Energies (Bhargava Vaidarbhi's Question)
"How many distinct energies sustain the human frame, and which among them is foremost?"

The sage answered: Space, Air, Fire, Water, Earth, Speech, Sight, and Hearing maintain the body. Once,
these faculties grew proud, claiming superiority. Prana, the master life-breath, demonstrated the truth: as
Prana rose to depart, all other senses were pulled out helpless; as Prana settled, all senses returned. Just
as bees follow the queen bee when she leaves the hive, all senses depend entirely on Prana.

3. The Movement of Prana (Kausalya Asvalayana's Question)
"From where is this Prana born, and how does it divide itself to govern the body?"

Pippalada explained: Prana is born directly from the Self (Atman); just as a person casts a shadow, Prana
is the projection of the Soul. It allocates five functional branches:

    Prana: Operates in the eyes, ears, nose, and mouth, governing respiration and sensory intake.
    Apana: Operates in the lower organs, governing elimination and reproductive functions.
    Samana: Operates in the gastric center, digesting food and balancing nutrients.
    Vyana: Operates throughout the network of 101 subtle nadis, circulating vitality.
    Udana: Ascends through the crown of the head via the Sushumna nadi at the moment of death,
    guiding the soul to higher realms according to its mental purity and deeds.

4. Sleep and the Witnessing Consciousness (Sauryayani Gargya's Question)
"Which faculties sleep within man? Which remain awake? Who experiences dreams and deep bliss?"

When the Sun sets, its rays gather into its luminous disc; upon dawn, they stream outward again.
Similarly, during sleep, the five senses withdraw into the mind. The senses sleep, but the five fires of Prana
remain awake within the body. In the dream state, the mind wanders through remembered impressions.When the mind is absorbed into pure consciousness, deep dreamless sleep (Sushupti) takes place, and the
soul rests in unalloyed peace.

5. Contemplation of OM (Satyakama Saibya's Question)
"What realm is attained by one who meditates upon the syllable OM until death?"

OM represents both the relative and the absolute Brahman. Chanting one matra ('A') brings return to
human birth with nobility. Chanting two matras ('U') elevates the soul to the intermediate lunar plane.
Meditating upon all three matras ('A-U-M') unifies the soul with solar consciousness, freeing it from all
impurities just as a snake sheds its slough, leading to the supreme abode of Brahman.

6. The Being of Sixteen Portals (Sukesha Bharadwaja's Question)
"Where resides the Supreme Person endowed with sixteen kalas (faculties)?"

Pippalada replied: That Supreme Person dwells right here, within the physical body. From the Self arise
the sixteen kalas: Prana, Faith, Space, Air, Fire, Water, Earth, Senses, Mind, Food, Vigor, Austerity,
Mantras, Deeds, Worlds, and Name. Just as flowing rivers lose their distinct names and shapes upon
entering the ocean, these sixteen divisions dissolve entirely when the Self is realized. The realized sage
attains freedom from mortality.

Positioned as the concluding section of the Taittiriya Aranyaka in the Krishna Yajurveda, this text is also
termed the Yagnopanishad because it details the inner spiritual dimensions of sacrificial worship and
prayer.


COSMIC GENESIS & THE PRIMORDIAL PERSON

From the unmanifest Supreme Brahman, the cosmic order unfolded: Space, Air, Fire, Water, Earth,
plants, food, and living creatures. The Pancha Bhutas constitute the physical vehicle for all life. The
Supreme Person is subtler than the subtlest and vaster than the vastest, seated secretly within the heart of
every being. The universe moves under His cosmic governance, depicted as the Cosmic Person with
thousands of eyes, thousands of arms, and thousands of feet.


SACRED INVOCATIONS & NATURE WORSHIP

The text honors cosmic forces through revered Vedic hymns:

   Surya & Gayatri: The Sun God is invoked to burn away moral impurities and illumine human
   intelligence. The divine mother Durga (Parashakti) is prayed to for the protection of society, crops, and
   families.
   Agni (The Sacrificial Flame): Revered in seven expressions, including Vaisvanaragni (the
   digestive hearth that transforms nourishment into vital life).
   Bhudevi (The Mother Earth): Bearing the footsteps of life, the soil is praised before undertaking
   agriculture; touching earth to the head serves as an act of purification.


INTERNAL SACRIFICIAL ARCHITECTURE

The external Vedic altar requires purifying the site with cow-dung, drawing designs, laying bricks
squarely, and kindling wooden billets (Samidhas). Clarified butter (Ghee) and consecrated offerings
(Havis) are poured into the flames while priests chant sacred mantras to invoke divine grace.

The Upanishad translates this ritual into an inward spiritual practice:

   The physical body is the sacrificial altar.
   Daily activities, speech, breath, and thoughts are the offerings poured into the inward fire.
   The five pranas (Prana, Apana, Vyana, Udana, Samana) are purified.
   Ego, pride, malice, and anger are consumed within the fire of self-awareness.


THE MEDITATION ON NARAYANA IN THE HEART LOTUS

Within the center of the chest dwells the heart, resembling an inverted lotus bud. At its center burns a
blazing, smokeless flame of consciousness. Within that golden center abides the Supreme Being—
Narayana, Shiva, Hari, and Indra—the changeless ruler of all.The scripture resolves all sectarian differences, declaring: "Shiva is Vishnu, and Vishnu is Shiva" (Yatha
Shivamayo Vishnuh, Evam Vishnumayah Shivah). They are non-different expressions of the one supreme
reality.


THE SYNTHESIS OF SPIRITUAL VALUES

The Upanishad concludes with an inventory of human virtues:

   Truth (Satyam) is supreme.
   Inner discipline (Tapas) is essential.
   Sensory restraint (Damam) and mental calmness (Samam) are indispensable.
   Selfless charity (Danam) is the crown of virtues.
   Righteous conduct (Dharma) upholds society.
   Renunciation (Nyasa / Sanyasa) is the highest path to direct liberation.

The fruits of selfless, noble deeds spread sweetness like blooming flowers across the world, while
unrighteous actions bring distress. Purifying the mind through service, meditation, and wisdom allows the
soul to transcend birth and death.
