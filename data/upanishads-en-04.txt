                               6. AITAREYOPANISHAD

Preserved within the second section of the Aitareya Aranyaka of the Rigveda, this scripture consists of 33
verses revealed through Sage Mahidasa Aitareya. It explains cosmic genesis, the incarnation of consciousness
within the human frame, and delivers the foundational Mahavakya: "Prajnanam Brahma".

PEACE INVOCATION (SANTHI MANTRA)


  "Om! May my speech be firmly grounded in my mind; may my mind be established in my speech. O
  Self-effulgent Reality, reveal Thyself unto me! May the wisdom of the Vedic teachings never desert me.
  May I speak the truth; may I live the truth. May That protect me; may That protect my preceptor! Om
  Peace! Peace! Peace!"


COSMOGENESIS AND EMBODIMENT

   The Projection of the Spheres: In the beginning, the Atman alone existed; there was nothing else
   active. The Self willed: "Let Me create the worlds." He manifested five cosmic realms: Ambhas (the high
   celestial waters), Swarga (the luminous heavens), Marichi (the solar atmosphere), Mara (the mortal
   earth), and Apas (the subterranean foundation).
   The Manifestation of the Cosmic Person: From the cosmic waters, He condensed the archetypal
   Purusha. From the Purusha's mouth, speech and fire emerged; from the nostrils, breath and air; from the
   eyes, sight and the Sun; from the ears, hearing and the cardinal directions; from the skin, hair and
   vegetation; from the heart, mind and the Moon; and from the navel, the descending breath (Apana) and
   physical death.
   The Entry of Consciousness: To sustain the living forms, food was generated. The Supreme Divine
   then entered the human structure through the crown aperture of the skull, known as the
   Brahmarandhra (Vidriti).

The body possesses 101 primary subtle conduits converging at the heart, with one major channel ascending to
the crown. When an individual's departing prana exits through the Brahmarandhra, liberation from rebirth is
attained.

Just as dream perceptions dissolve instantly upon awakening, worldly delusions vanish when the Soul is
realized. The Sun illuminates human activity while remaining uninvolved; similarly, the Atman activates all
bodily and intellectual functions without being conditioned by them.

THE GREAT DECLARATION: PRAJNANAM BRAHMA

What is this Self? It is pure Consciousness—the capacity by which one sees, hears, smells, articulates, and
discerns. Perception, intellect, insight, wisdom, memory, and will are all functional names of Consciousness.
The universe is sustained and guided by Consciousness. Consciousness is Brahman!

10    Mahanarayanopanishad                50                             6. TAITTIRIYOPANISHAD

In the Krishna Yajurveda, within the Taittiriya Aranyaka, a significant section is known as the
Taittiriyopanishad. The disciples of Sage Vaisampayana received these sacred mantras by assuming the
forms of Tittiri birds; hence, it was given the title of Taittiriyopanishad.

This scripture expounds three foundational disciplines:

 1. Siksha Valli (Jeevana Vidya): The science of living, educating man on how to conduct his daily life
    in this world.
 2. Ananda Valli (Manishi): The profound self-inquiry exploring "Who am I?" and the architecture of
    the human personality.
 3. Bhrigu Valli (The God): The supreme inquiry and realization of the ultimate Divine nature.

The foundation for all human attainment is studentship (Brahmacharya). It is during youth that an
individual determines his lifestyle to accomplish life's highest targets. This Upanishad advises students:
"Do not let life drift aimlessly along its own chaotic course; take charge and live your life intentionally."


FOUNDATIONS OF FORMATIVE EDUCATION

 1. Ninety percent of the human brain develops by the sixth year of age. That is why young children
    constantly question everything around them. Recognizing this natural milestone, our ancient elders
    established the sixth year as the ideal time to initiate formal education.
 2. Impressions imprinted upon a child's mind at that tender age are never erased. Consequently, the
    conduct between father and mother must remain deeply harmonious, polite, and rooted in humanity.
    Parents must cultivate wholesome habits, refined language, respect, and reverence by narrating stories
    from the Ramayana and classic moral literature.
 3. Every human being receives a natural, God-given talent by birth—whether in vocal music, painting,
    poetry, writing, crafts, or sciences. Parents and teachers must identify this latent inclination early and
    place the child under an able preceptor to draw out their highest potential.
