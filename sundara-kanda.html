Sundara Kanda

Hanuma's heroic journey to Lanka and Sita.

Illustrated overview from the source manuscript.

← All six Kandas
RAMAYANAM

Sundara Kanda

❈

Valmiki Maharshi titled this section Sundara Kanda (the beautiful chapter) Because, while Rama plays no direct physical role, Hanuma and Sita remain immersed In devotion (Rama-upasana), culminating in spiritual beauty.

Hanuma sprang from Mount Mahendra with a mighty leap that pressed the mountain Deep into the earth. Saluting his guru, the Sun God, he sped over the sea.

The Sea God requested Mount Mainaka to rise from the waters to offer Hanuma Rest. Hanuma affectionately tapped the mountain peak, declining to stop until Rama’s mission was accomplished.

Next, Surasa, the mother of Nagas, blocked his path in a monstrous form, claiming He must enter her mouth. Hanuma expanded his size; Surasa widened her maw Accordingly. In a flash, Hanuma contracted to the size of a thumb, darted into Her cavernous mouth, and exited, fulfilling the boon while demonstrating his Sharp intellect.

Further along, the demoness Simhika grasped his shadow from beneath the waves. Hanuma dove into her mouth, ripped her vitals, emerged victorious, and landed On the coast of Lanka.

Hanuma reduced his form to the size of a cat and approached the outer walls of Lanka at dusk. Lankini, the guardian deity of the city, barred his entrance and Struck him. Hanuma dealt her a fist blow, felling her. Remembering Brahma’s Prophecy that her defeat by a vanara heralded the destruction of Lanka, Lankini Yielded. Placing his left foot forward—an omen presaging the doom of the Household—Hanuma breached the walls.

He searched through lavish mansions, finding intoxicated rakshasas, and entered Ravana’s palace. He observed Ravana sleeping on a golden couch, his queen Mandodari, and thousands of royal consorts. A consummate celibate (Brahmachari), Hanuma maintained complete equanimity throughout. He searched the Pushpaka Vimanam, but could not find Sita.

Praying fervently to Rama, Lakshmana, and Sita, Hanuma ventured toward the Asoka Vanam. Moving through the trees, he spotted a lean, tearful woman sitting beneath A Simsupa tree, draped in an unwashed silk saree, surrounded by hideous Demonesses. She continuously murmured the name of Rama. Hanuma recognized her as Sita Devi.

During the sacred Brahmi muhurtam, Ravana arrived in regal attire with a retinue Of women. Ravana boasted of his treasures, belittled Rama as an impoverished Wanderer who could never cross the ocean, and pleaded for her affection.

Sita placed a blade of straw between them as a barrier and spoke fearlessly. She Stated that she would never address an unrelated male directly. She declared that Just as light cannot be separated from the sun, she belonged solely to Rama. She Warned Ravana to return her to Rama and seek pardon, or face total annihilation.

Enraged, Ravana brandished his sword, giving her two months to submit, and ordered The demoness guards to torment her into compliance. Consort Dhanyamalini Intervened, coaxing Ravana back to his chambers.

The rakshasis—including Krijata and Ekajata—threatened to tear Sita to pieces. The elderly demoness Trijata silenced them and narrated an ominous dream: • Sri Rama, effulgent on a chariot drawn by a thousand swans and riding an Elephant with four tusks, reunited with Sita. • Ravana, bathed in oil, dressed in black, garlanded with oleander flowers, Tumbled headlong south on a donkey cart, dragged toward darkness by a spectral Figure. • A great monkey set fire to Lanka, reducing its palaces to cinders.

Trijata cautioned the guards to venerate Sita to avert doom.

Sita, overwhelmed by despair, attempted to hang herself from a Simsupa branch Using her long tresses. Observing from above, Hanuma gently began narrating the Lineage of King Dasaradha, the noble deeds of Rama, their exile, the loss of Sita, And Rama’s pact with Sugriva.

Startled by the sweet narration, Sita looked up and saw a small monkey perched Among the branches. Hanuma descended reverently, maintained a respectful Distance, and spoke with profound humility. To dispel her lingering fear that he Was an illusion created by Ravana, Hanuma detailed Rama’s physical appearance and Divine qualities from head to toe. He presented Sri Rama’s signet ring (Rama-nama Mudrika).

Sita received the ring with tears of joy. Hanuma offered to carry her across the Ocean on his back, expanding his body to mountainous proportions to prove his Ability. Sita commended his valor but declined, affirming her vow never to Willingly touch any male other than Rama, adding that Rama himself must cross the Sea and vanquish Ravana to preserve the honor of the Raghu dynasty.

To validate his meeting with Rama, Sita recounted the private episode of Kakasura at Chitrakuta. When Indra’s son, disguised as a crow, attacked her, Rama Released a blade of energized grass with the Brahmastra, pursuing the bird across The worlds until it surrendered at Rama’s feet, losing an eye as symbolic Expiation. Sita then gave Hanuma her crest jewel, the Chudamani, as an Unmistakable token.

Receiving the Chudamani, Hanuma resolved to test the enemy’s strength. He uprooted The pleasure gardens of the Pramadavanam. Ravana dispatched eighty thousand Kinkara warriors; Hanuma battered them using an iron beam from the gates. He slew General Jambumali, the seven sons of ministers, and five great army commanders. When Ravana sent his younger son Akshakumara, Hanuma crushed him after an intense Duel.

Ravana then dispatched his eldest son, Indrajit. Indrajit invoked the Brahmastra, And Hanuma willingly allowed himself to be bound, yielding to the weapon’s Sanctity to gain audience with Ravana. Brought to the assembly hall, Hanuma Introduced himself as the humble envoy of Sri Rama. He urged Ravana to return Sita honorably, reminding him how Lord Shiva crushed his hands beneath Mount Kailash, and how Vali once bound him in his tail and dipped him in the four Oceans.

Ravana ordered Hanuma executed. Vibhishana intervened, pointing out that scriptures Forbid the execution of an envoy. Ravana instead ordered Hanuma’s tail wrapped in Cloth, soaked in oil, and set ablaze.

As the tail burned, Sita prayed to Agni Deva that the flame remain cool to Hanuma. Hanuma shrank his form, slipped out of his bonds, leaped across the roofs Of Lanka, and set the city ablaze. He quenched his tail in the sea, verified Sita’s Safety under the Simsupa tree, and leaped back across the waters to Mount Mahendra.

The southern search party celebrated at the Madhuvana and returned to Prasravana. Hanuma bowed before Sri Rama and uttered the words: “Seen have I Sita” (Kanditi Seetanu), placing the Chudamani into Rama’s trembling hands.
