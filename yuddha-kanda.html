Yuddha Kanda

By Mr. K.V.V Subramanyam

RAMAYANAM: YUDDHA KANDA

Rama felt very happy of Hanuma going to Lanka, finding Sita, and assuring her That Rama would come and take her. Saying that he had nothing material to offer Hanuma, Rama hugged him affectionately. Hanuma briefed Rama on Lanka’s fortifications, Moats, and garrison strengths. Selecting the auspicious noon hour under the Vaisakha star, Rama ordered the vanara army to march south. Rama rode on Hanuma’s Shoulders and Laxmana on Angada’s, reaching the southern shores by nightfall.

In Lanka, Ravana held a war council. Sycophantic ministers praised his past Conquests, while Indrajit boasted of his magical warfare. Vibhishana alone Advocated dharma, urging the return of Sita to prevent absolute doom. Kumbhakarna Criticized Ravana for acting without foresight, but pledged his brute strength For the battle. When Vibhishana persisted with righteous counsel, Ravana kicked Him in fury and banished him.

Vibhishana flew across the ocean with four loyal counselors and sought refuge (Saranagati) at Rama’s camp. Vanara generals counseled suspicion, but Hanuma Supported accepting him, noting Vibhishana’s sincere demeanor. Rama declared his Eternal vow: he would grant sanctuary to anyone who approached him sincerely, Even Ravana himself. Rama performed Vibhishana’s consecration (Pattabhishekam) as The prospective King of Lanka.

To cross the ocean, Rama sat in meditation on a sacred grass mat on the seashore For three days. When Samudrudu (the Sea God) failed to appear, Rama strung an arrow And invoked the Brahmastra to dry the waters. Samudrudu rose from the depths with Folded hands, suggesting that Nala, son of the divine architect Viswakarma, supervise The construction of a stone bridge (Setu). At Samudrudu’s request, Rama directed His loaded shaft north to Dhrumakulya, cleansing the region of vile marauders and Turning it into a fertile oasis.

Led by Nala, millions of vanaras collected rocks and timber, completing the Hundred-yojana bridge within five days. The allied armies crossed into Lanka and Besieged the fortress.

Ravana attempted psychological warfare. His sorcerer Vidyujjihva created an Illusory severed head of Rama and his bow, which Ravana displayed to Sita. Sita Wept, but Vibhishana’s wife Sarama consoled her, revealing the illusion.

Rama sent Angada as an envoy to give Ravana one final opportunity to surrender, But Ravana ordered Angada seized. Angada leaped into the sky, smashing the palace Towers, and returned.

The battle commenced. The vanaras assaulted the ramparts with boulders and tree Trunks, while the rakshasas deployed arrows, lances, and mystical weapons. Indrajit entered the fray invisibly and discharged the Nagastram, binding Rama And Laxmana in venomous serpent coils. As the brothers lay motionless, Ravana Flew Sita over the battlefield in the Pushpaka Vimanam to witness the scene. Trijata reassured Sita that the divine craft would not fly carrying a widow. Garuda Arrived, scattering the serpent bonds instantly; his divine presence revived Rama And Laxmana with doubled strength.

Ravana then dispatched his fiercest generals:

* Dhumraksha was slain by Hanuma.

* Vajradamstra was killed by Angada.

* Akampana fell to Hanuma.

* Commander Prahasta was slain by Neela.

Ravana entered the battlefield in his grand chariot. Rama rode into combat upon Hanuma’s shoulders. In the fierce duel that followed, Rama shattered Ravana’s Chariot, snapped his bow, and cleaved his royal crown, an encounter celebrated As the Makuta Bhanga Sarga. Seeing Ravana weaponless, Rama spared him, saying, “Go today, you are exhausted; return tomorrow well-prepared”.

Humiliated, Ravana ordered his giant brother Kumbhakarna awakened. Demon troops Beat drums, blew trumpets, drove elephants over his body, and offered heaps of Meat and pots of wine. Upon waking, Kumbhakarna censured Ravana’s conduct, but Marched to battle out of familial loyalty. He trampled vanaras, caught Sugriva, And battered commanders. Sugriva bit off his nose and ears. Ultimately, Rama Stepped forward, severing Kumbhakarna’s arms, legs, and neck with divine weapons, Sending his colossal remains into the ocean.

Indrajit returned to the battle, deploying illusions and discharging the Brahmastra, Rendering Rama, Laxmana, and the vanara hosts unconscious. Jambavan instructed Hanuma to fly to the Himalayas and fetch four herbs: Mruta Sanjivani, Vishalya Karini, Sandhana Karini, and Savarnya Karini. Unable to locate the herbs in haste, Hanuma lifted the entire Dronagiri mountain and brought it back. The aroma of The divine herbs restored Rama, Laxmana, and all fallen vanaras to full health.

Indrajit next conjured a phantom Sita (Maya Sita) and severed her head before Hanuma’s eyes. Vibhishana clarified that it was a ruse to distract them while Indrajit completed the invincible Nikumbhila Yagna. Laxmana, guided by Vibhishana And guarded by Hanuma, interrupted the ritual. After a grueling three-day duel, Laxmana invoked the righteous name of Rama and severed Indrajit’s head with an Aindrastra.

Grief-stricken, Ravana rushed with drawn sword to execute Sita, but was dissuaded By his minister Mahaparsva. Mobilizing his remaining hosts, Ravana entered the Battlefield for the final showdown.

During the battle, Ravana hurled his spear (Shakti) at Vibhishana; Laxmana Shielded Vibhishana, taking the hit on his own chest, and collapsed. Hanuma flew To the Himalayas a second time to fetch the life-giving Sanjivani herb, reviving Laxmana.

Indra sent his divine golden chariot and charioteer Matali to assist Rama. As Rama severed Ravana’s heads, new heads sprouted in their place. Sage Agastya Descended and initiated Rama into the sacred hymn Aditya Hrudaya Stotram, written By Valmiki in thirty-one verses to invoke the Sun God. Rama recited the hymn thrice, Replenishing his spiritual energy.

Matali reminded Rama of the ultimate Brahmastra given by Sage Agastya, fashioned With the fury of all elements. Rama invoked the mantra and discharged the shaft.

It pierced Ravana’s chest, returned to Rama’s quiver, and the demon king fell Lifeless.

Mandodari wept bitterly over Ravana’s body, reflecting that his inability to Master his senses caused his doom, recognizing Rama as Lord Vishnu. Following Rama’s counsel, Vibhishana performed the obsequies, after which he was crowned King of Lanka.

Hanuma conveyed the news to Sita. Bathed and adorned, Sita arrived before Rama. To safeguard her honor against slanderous societal gossip, Rama required an Ordeal. Sita asked Laxmana to kindle a sacrificial fire and entered the flames. Agni Deva emerged carrying Sita unharmed, testifying to her purity. Rama received Her with joy, explaining that the ordeal was necessary to establish her Unimpeachable virtue before the world.

Lord Shiva, Brahma, and King Dasaradha (in celestial form) manifested from the Heavens to bless Rama and Sita. At Rama’s request, Indra revived all vanaras who Perished in the battle.

Rama, Sita, Laxmana, Sugriva, Vibhishana, and the vanaras boarded the Pushpaka Vimanam and flew northward toward Ayodhya. Stopping briefly at Kishkinda to pick Up Tara and Ruma, they reached Sage Bharadwaja’s ashram. Rama sent Hanuma ahead to Nandigram to inform Bharata of their arrival.

At Nandigram, Bharata joyfully handed the kingdom back to Rama. In Ayodhya, Sri Rama’s coronation (Pattabhishekam) was performed with holy waters from four oceans And five hundred rivers. Bharata was installed as Crown Prince (Yuvaraja). Rama Distributed gifts, presenting silk garments to Sugriva and Vibhishana, and a pair Of white silks and Sita’s pearl necklace to Hanuma.

Under Ramrajya, the earth flourished with three rains every month, bountiful Harvests, and peace.

RAMAYANA PHALITAM: Whoever listens to the Ramayana will enjoy a peaceful, virtuous life, good fortune, And noble partnerships.

Spiritual Essence (as taught by Sri Sri Sri Prabhakar Swamiji):

* Our body is Lanka

* Sita Mata is the soul (Atma)

* Ravana is the egoistic mind

* Ten heads of Ravana are the ten sense organs

* Hanuma is divine knowledge

* Slaying Ravana is conquering the mind, reuniting the soul with the Supreme Divine (Rama)
