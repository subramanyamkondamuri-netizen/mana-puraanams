Ramayanam · Kanda 3

Aranya Kanda

By Mr. K.V.V Subramanyam

RAMAYANAM: ARANYA KANDA

Aranya Kanda shows us that Yogam gives great knowledge, leading to life Benefits. Yogam means interaction with noble people.

Sri Rama visited a number of Tapovanams and interacted with great Rishis and Maharshis. Rama killed rakshasas, some of whom were released from earlier curses And turned back into Gandharvas.

Sita, Rama, and Laxmana went to a Tapovanam in the Dandakaranyam. There, they Heard the echo of Vedic chanting. The Rishis, seeing Rama in hermit attire, Were pleased by his grace. They requested him to protect them from rakshasas as Their king, since one-sixth of their tapophalam goes to the king, similar to Taxes paid by citizens. Rama assured them that he would slay the rakshasas.

Venturing further into the forest, they noticed flies called cheakuru vayuvalu Buzzing over traces of blood—a sign of an ogre’s presence where even tigers and Lions feared to tread. A massive ogre appeared with blood smeared around his Mouth. On his shoulder was a long spear carrying three tigers, four lions, two Wolves, deer carcasses, and an elephant’s head. He attacked Rama and Laxmana, Claiming he could not be killed by arrows. When Rama shot an astra at the ogre, Named Viradhudu, he collapsed. Rama and Laxmana struck him with blows and kicks, Pinning his arms back. Rama held him down with a foot on his neck and asked Laxmana to dig a deep pit to bury Viradhudu. The ogre recognized that Sita, Rama, And Laxmana had arrived to release him from his curse. Upon being buried, he Transformed back into a celestial being named Tumburu.

He advised Rama to visit Sarabhanga Maharshi Asram for their welfare. Sita, Rama, And Laxmana proceeded there. They saw the Maharshi conversing with Indra, who Hovered in mid-air in his chariot. Upon seeing Rama, Indra departed in his Chariot.

Sarabhanga, the aged sage, told Rama that he had been waiting for his arrival. He offered all his tapophalam to Rama. Rama declined, asking instead for a Suitable location to erect his parnasala to earn his own taposhakti. The Maharshi Directed Rama to Sutikshna Maharshi’s hermitage.

Then Sarabhanga Maharshi lit the Yagna Vedi, pouring ghee to raise high flames. He entered the flames, and his soul ascended to the Urdhva Lokas, high above the Heavens. Brahma Deva stood and welcomed Sarabhanga Maharshi into Satya Lokam, All of which Rama witnessed.

Rama, Sita, and Laxmana then proceeded to Sutikshna Maharshi Asram. Sutikshna Also mentioned that Indra had invited him, but he had waited to see Rama. Other Hermits gathered to appeal to Rama for protection against ogres. Rama gave his Word to destroy the ogres.

Sutikshna Maharshi offered his own tapobalam to Rama, which Rama politely Declined, seeking instead guidance on where to build a hermitage. The sage Suggested touring the surrounding Tapomandalams and returning.

Sita posed a philosophical question to Rama regarding his pledges. She observed That three harmful traits arise from kama (desire):

1. Speaking falsehood.

2. Coveting women other than one’s wife.

3. Committing violence without direct enmity.

She affirmed that Rama and Laxmana were entirely free of the first two traits, But noted that Rama’s promise to kill the ogres, who had not directly wronged Him, bordered on the third. Rama explained that protection was his duty as a Kshatriya king, especially since one-sixth of the sages’ spiritual merit flows to The ruler who defends them. Sita agreed with his reasoning.

They traveled to Abhayaranyam, spending one year, two years, or several months Across various hermitages, completing ten years of their exile. They returned to Sutikshna Maharshi, who directed Rama to Agastya Maharshi’s brother’s hermitage, And then to Agastya Maharshi himself.

They stayed one night at the brother’s hermitage. It was the site where Agastya Had digested the demon Vatapi, who used to slay innocent brahmins.

Proceeding to Agastya Maharshi Asram, the great sage received them warmly.

Rama observed that even deities visited the sage for spiritual guidance. Agastya Presented Rama with golden-hilted swords, the divine Vishnu Dhanussu, and two Akshaya Tunheeralu (inexhaustible quivers whose arrows replenish automatically After use). He advised them to build a hermitage at Panchavati on the banks of The Godavari river, near Rishyamuka mountain.

At Panchavati, Laxmana erected a neat parnasala. During chilly winter mornings, Sita, Rama, and Laxmana took holy dips in the Godavari river daily.

One day, while Rama and Sita sat outside the parnasala, Surpanakha, a rakshasi, Arrived. She expressed romantic interest in Rama, declaring her lineage as the Sister of Ravana, Kumbhakarna, and Vibhishana. She proposed marrying Rama and Threatened to devour Sita and Laxmana. Rama jestingly replied that he was already Married and directed her to Laxmana, calling him handsome and unattached in the Forest. Laxmana playfully redirected her back to Rama.

Enraged, she lunged at Sita to kill her. Rama ordered Laxmana to punish her, And Laxmana sliced off her nose and ears. Bleeding and wailing, she fled to her Local kin, Khara and Dushana.

Khara dispatched fourteen elite warriors to kill Rama and Laxmana, but Rama Eliminated them with swift arrows. Surpanakha returned to Khara in despair. Khara then mobilized his commanders and an army of fourteen thousand warriors To wage open combat against Rama. Bad omens appeared for Khara: red rain fell, Vultures perched on his standard, and jackals howled; yet he ignored them.

Rama noticed auspicious war omens: his Kodandam twitched and sweet smoke curled From his quivers. He instructed Laxmana to escort Sita to a secure mountain cave Nearby.

Khara’s forces launched an assault on Rama, who stood alone. Wielding the Vishnu Dhanussu given by Agastya, Rama unleashed a torrent of arrows, wiping out the Entire fourteen-thousand-strong host in one hour and ten minutes, slaying Khara And Dushana.

Devatas and Rishis watched his martial prowess in awe. Sita emerged safely from The cave, running to Rama and embracing him from all sides in joy, relieving him Of all battle fatigue.

An ogre named Akampanudu witnessed Rama’s might from hiding and fled to Lanka To apprise Ravana. When Ravana vowed to kill Rama, Akampanudu warned that Rama Could not be defeated easily in combat and suggested abducting his beloved wife, Sita.

Surpanakha also arrived at Ravana’s court, detailing Sita’s beauty and Instigating Ravana to claim her as his queen, blaming Rama and Laxmana for her Mutilation.

Ravana visited Maricha’s hermitage for assistance. Maricha strongly warned Ravana against crossing Rama, stating it would bring absolute ruin to Lanka. Ravana temporarily relented and returned.

Later, egged on again by Surpanakha’s vivid praises of Sita’s beauty, Ravana’s Desire reignited. He dismissed his ministers and flew by aerial chariot to Maricha once more. By then, Maricha had turned ascetic, constantly chanting Rama’s name; fear of Rama had transformed into continuous devotion.

Despite Maricha’s pleadings, Ravana ordered him to assume the form of an alluring Golden deer with silver spots and wander before Sita’s hermitage. The plan was To lure Rama far away into the forest, mimic Rama’s voice calling for help (“Ha Sita! Ha Laxmana!”), cause Laxmana to leave Sita unprotected, and allow Ravana to abduct her. Maricha warned that Rama would kill him, but Ravana Threatened him with immediate death if he refused. Choosing death at the hands Of Rama, Maricha transformed into a radiant golden deer.

The deer gamboled playfully around Sita as she gathered flowers. Enchanted, she Called Rama and Laxmana, pleading to capture the deer to take back to Ayodhya After their exile. Laxmana warned that it was an illusion orchestrated by Maricha. Rama decided that if it was an ordinary animal he would bring it, and if it was a Demon in disguise he would eliminate it. Leaving Laxmana on high alert to guard Sita, Rama chased the creature.

The deer led Rama deep into the forest. Realizing it could not be captured alive, Rama released a deadly arrow. As the shaft struck, Maricha reverted to his true Demonic form and cried out in Rama’s voice: “Haa Sita! Haa Laxmana!” before dying.

Hearing the cry, Sita panicked, believing Rama was in mortal peril. Laxmana Insisted that no power could harm Rama and that the voice was fraudulent demon Trickery. Distraught, Sita leveled harsh allegations at Laxmana, accusing him of Harboring improper motives and waiting for Rama’s demise.

Deeply wounded, Laxmana invoked the forest deities (Vana Devatas) to witness his Innocence and the unjust accusations that forced him to leave. Saluting Sita’s Feet, he departed reluctantly, walking backwards and looking back vigilantly to Protect her from afar.

Once Laxmana vanished from sight, Ravana disguised himself as a mendicant Sanyasi and approached the parnasala seeking alms. Believing him to be an Ascetic, Sita offered him water and hospitality. Ravana dropped his guise, Praised her beauty extravagantly, and demanded she leave with him to become the Chief queen of Lanka. Sita rebuked him fiercely, declaring that Ravana was an Insignificant insect compared to the lion-like Rama.

Infuriated, Ravana revealed his ten heads and twenty arms. He seized Sita by her Hair and dragged her into his chariot. Clamping her onto his chariot, he soared Into the sky as she wept and cried out for Rama.

Jatayuvu, the aged king of birds and friend of Dasaradha, heard her cries and Intercepted Ravana in mid-air. Jatayuvu shattered Ravana’s bow, killed his Chariot mules and charioteer, and tore off ten of Ravana’s arms. Ravana regenerated His limbs, drew his sword, and severed Jatayuvu’s wings and talons, leaving the Bird mortally wounded.

As Ravana flew southward, Sita noticed five vanaras on a hilltop. She tore a piece Of her upper garment, tied some of her ornaments into a bundle, and dropped it Among them. Ravana flew across the ocean and interned Sita in his palace under The strict guard of terrifying rakshasis. He ordered that anyone entering Without permission would be put to death, and dispatched ten spies to monitor Rama’s movements in Dandakaranyam.

Ravana tried tempting Sita by showing his opulent palace, gold pillars embedded With gems, and the Pushpaka Vimanam taken from Kubera. Sita shut her eyes and Held her saree before her face, warning him that his pride, wealth, and life Would be reduced to ashes by Rama’s wrath. Losing patience, Ravana ordered her Removal to the Asoka Vanam under a Simsupa tree, guarded by demonesses instructed To alternate between threats and cajoling. He gave her an ultimatum of twelve Months, warning she would be butchered for his morning meal if she did not yield.

Returning from Maricha’s hunt, Rama noticed ill omens: a jackal cried and deer Bounded across his path. Meeting Laxmana on the trail, Rama was distressed and Admonished him for leaving Sita alone. They rushed back to the hermitage and found It deserted.

Rama collapsed in grief, questioning the trees, the Pancha Bhutas, the deer, and The river Godavari, who remained silent out of fear of Ravana. Moving south, the Brothers discovered signs of struggle: crushed flowers from Sita’s hair, broken Fragments of Ravana’s chariot, fallen ornaments, and dead mules.

They encountered the dying Jatayuvu soaked in blood. Jatayuvu informed them that Ravana had abducted Sita and severed his wings when he tried to intervene. Having Imparted this information, Jatayuvu breathed his last in Rama’s arms. Rama and Laxmana cremated the bird with the full funeral honors accorded to an elder father.

Entering the dense Krouncha forest, they were accosted by a demoness named Ayomukhi, whose nose, ears, and breasts Laxmana cut off when she attempted to Seize him. Moving forward, they encountered Kabandha, a headless monstrosity Whose face and single eye were in his belly, equipped with enormous arms spanning Miles. Kabandha seized both brothers. On Rama’s cue, they severed his arms Simultaneously.

Released, Kabandha revealed his former identity as a celestial Gandharva named Danu, cursed by a sage and deformed by Indra’s thunderbolt. He told them that Burning his body on a pyre would restore his divine form, after which he would Guide them. Following his cremation, the resplendent Gandharva advised Rama to Proceed to Rishyamuka Mountain and forge an alliance with Sugriva, the exiled Vanara chief living with his four ministers, including Hanuma.

On their journey, Rama visited the hermitage of Sabari, an aged, devoted Disciple of Sage Matanga. She offered Rama fresh wild berries and displayed her Spiritual accomplishments, pointing out that the sacrificial altars and flowers Left by her departed gurus remained fresh after thirteen years. Having attained Rama’s holy darshan, Sabari entered a consecrated fire and ascended in a radiant Celestial form.

Rama and Laxmana continued to Lake Pampa, where Rama bathed, finding solace Before setting out to meet Sugriva.
